## Slide 5
5 A Case Study on Revolution Medicines and the Parallels to Dialectic Therapeutics: On April 14, 2026, Revolution Medicines (Nasdaq: RVMD) reported topline data that set a new standard of care in metastatic pancreatic cancer, nearly doubling survival in second-line PDAC against a target the field had spent thirty years calling undruggable. The market had already priced the conviction - $19B in market cap built before the readout ($32B as of May 2026), by investors who saw the setup early: a difficult target, a modality shift, preclinical regressions where prior generations had failed, and clinical confirmation that followed. RAS had defeated direct inhibition for three decades. The protein cycles between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state, and prior programs tried to drug the inactive state - the structurally accessible one - but cancer cells signal through the active state. The drugs bound where the biology wasn’t. Revolution built tri-complex inhibitors that engage RAS in its active, GTP-bound, ON-state by recruiting a chaperone protein to create a binding pocket that doesn’t exist on RAS alone. This was the same target prior generations had pursued; the difference was a new modality that finally engaged it where it actually drives disease. Similarly, Dialectic is Leveraging an Innovative Approach to Target BCL-XL, Previously Considered Undruggable: BCL-XL is one of the most validated survival targets in oncology, and one of the most prized unmet opportunities in modern drug development. Navitoclax (Genentech’s program) and every next-generation BH3 mimetic failed for the same reason: BCL-XL keeps tumor cells alive, but it also keeps platelets alive. Inhibit one, and you bleed the patient. The field concluded that the target was a wall. The obstacle was the modality, rather than the target. A small-molecule inhibitor cannot distinguish BCL-XL in a tumor cell from BCL-XL in a platelet - the protein is identical in both. A degrader can, because it works through a different mechanism: it doesn’t block the protein, it destroys it, and the destruction machinery itself is what creates the selectivity. DT2216 is a PROTAC, a heterobifunctional molecule that brings two proteins together to trigger destruction of one of them. One end engages BCL-XL; the other end recruits VHL, an E3 ubiquitin ligase that marks proteins for proteasomal degradation. When DT2216 connects the two, BCL-XL is ubiquitinated and destroyed. VHL is abundantly expressed in tumor cells and nearly absent in platelets. The same molecule encounters the same protein in both cell types, but only in tumor cells is the disposal machinery present to act on it. This is the selectivity prior BCL-XL programs could not engineer. Inhibitors act on the target alone - the same protein in tumor cells and platelets, the same drug effect in both. Degraders act on the target through the cellular machinery, and that machinery exists in tumor cells but not in platelets. The selectivity is not in the molecule; it is in the biology that the molecule recruits. Experimental findings suggest that tissue-specific drug accumulation and retention drive efficacy, rather than plasma exposure, enabling less frequent dosing while maintaining therapeutic effects. A Novel Approach to Precision Targeting: Changing a Modality to Treat Previously Undruggable Targets

## Slide 11
PK/PD Disconnect of VHL-E3 Ligase Protein Degraders Tissue Retention : Prolonged retention in tissues, including tumors, resulting in sustained drug concentrations in target tissues even after plasma levels decline.  Tumor Growth Inhibition : In xenograft models, DT2216 achieves tumor growth inhibition with twice weekly or weekly IV dosing.  The tumor-to-blood drug concentration asymmetry highlights the disconnect between plasma levels and tissue efficacy. Cellular Retention : In vitro studies show DT2216 remains in cancer cells for extended periods, maintaining BCL-XL protein degradation even after extensive washout. This indicates prolonged intracellular activity independent of media concentrations.  Biomarker Correlation : The inverse correlation between plasma concentrations and the suppression of BCL-XL in PBMCs further supports the PK/PD disconnect.  Conclusions:  These findings suggest that tissue-specific drug accumulation and retention drive efficacy, rather than plasma exposure, enabling less frequent dosing while maintaining therapeutic effects. ## Slide 12
DT2216 vs. Navitoclax: Distinct Mechanism and Superior Safety Profile Navitoclax is a  small molecule inhibitor,  which continually inhibits BCL-XL  Drug concentration of Navitoclax in platelets is similar to tissue concentrations because of passive diffusion Navitoclax accumulates with daily dosing (plasma AUC increase 50-100%), due to its 17 hr t1/2 Platelets are continually exposed to higher levels of Navitoclax after the 1 st  week, leading to severe thrombocytopenia Navitoclax studies often employed drug holidays and dose reductions, which cause loss of efficacy DT2216 is a  protein degrader (PROTAC) Platelets have lower levels of the E3 ligase VHL and the E1 and E2 ligases required for protein degradation The short plasma half-life relative to the dosing interval results in a PK and PD dissociation with continued degradation of BCL-XL in tumor cells after the plasma concentration goes to zero. This results in a longer enzymatic-like MOA. New platelets released from megakaryocytes are generated and not exposed until the next dose DT2216 does not accumulate in plasma with twice weekly dosing, and reductions in platelets do not worsen beyond the drop after the first dose in preclinical safety studies and in our Phase 1 clinical trial 12

## Slide 13
DT2216 Exhibits Significant Tissue-to-Plasma Concentration Asymmetry Sustained Efficacy : DT2216's prolonged retention in tumors allows for sustained PD effects despite rapid clearance from plasma.  This supports less frequent dosing schedules (e.g., every 1-2/week), while maintaining therapeutic efficacy.  Targeted Tumor Inhibition : The higher drug concentrations in tumors compared to plasma enhance its ability to inhibit tumor growth, as demonstrated in xenograft models.  This asymmetry ensures effective drug delivery to target tissues. Reduced Toxicity : By focusing on tissue-specific accumulation, while minimizing systemic exposure ,  DT2216 has reduced potential for thrombocytopenia over occupancy-based BCL-XL inhibitors (e.g. navitoclax). Optimized Dosing Strategies : The tissue-to-plasma asymmetry enables the development of dosing regimens that prioritize tissue exposure over plasma pharmacokinetics, potentially improving patient compliance and therapeutic outcomes.  13
