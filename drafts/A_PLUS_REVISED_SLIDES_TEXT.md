Investment Summary
DT2216 plus weekly paclitaxel in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer
Clinical foundation. Phase 1 established 0.40 mg/kg IV twice weekly as the recommended Phase 2 dose and showed rapid BCL-XL reduction in peripheral leukocytes. Monotherapy activity in advanced solid tumors was limited. Combination rationale. Paclitaxel-induced mitotic arrest reduces MCL-1 and increases dependence on BCL-XL, which DT2216 is designed to degrade. Preclinical evidence. Seven of eight patient-derived ovarian models showed ex-vivo synergy. DT2216 plus paclitaxel produced durable control in both flank and disseminated DF83 PDX models. Clinical value inflection. The ongoing Phase 1b/2 study will test combination safety, dose, response rate, durability and tumor pharmacodynamics. Human combination efficacy has not yet been reported.

Why the combination is needed
BCL-XL targeting alone induces compensatory pro-survival signaling, including MCL-1. Direct BCL-XL and MCL-1 inhibition produced synergistic apoptosis in 88.6% of 132 tested lines, but was not tolerated in vivo. Paclitaxel supplies the MCL-1 effect in the current clinical combination.
This is the biological basis for the current ovarian clinical program.
Source: Qin et al., submitted manuscript, July 2026, Figs. 1–3. Direct dual-inhibitor data are mechanistic context, not the clinical regimen.

## Slide 22
DT2216 increased apoptosis with three microtubule ADC payloads in OVCAR8
The current clinical program remains DT2216 plus weekly paclitaxel. These data support future combination studies.
DT2216 combined with DM4, MMAE and MMAF increased apoptosis in OVCAR8 cells.
MMAF-induced apoptosis increased with BCL-XL inhibition or degradation. Selective BCL-2 or MCL-1 inhibition did not produce the same effect.
DM4 is the payload used by mirvetuximab soravtansine (Elahere). Free payload was tested; the intact ADC was not.

Safety and pharmacology
Thrombocytopenia occurred in 9 of 20 patients. One dose-limiting grade 4 event resolved within 48 hours. No major bleeding events, treatment-related deaths or platelet transfusions were reported. Plasma exposure was dose proportional, with no accumulation on twice-weekly dosing.
Antitumor activity
DT2216 did not demonstrate single-agent activity in advanced solid tumors. Stable disease was the best RECIST response in 4 of 20 patients, with a median duration of 107 days. The Phase 1 result supports development with a mechanism-matched combination rather than monotherapy.
Source: Mahadevan et al., J Hematol Oncol 2025; NCT04886622.

## Slide 27
Phase 1 established systemic pharmacodynamics; Phase 1b will test tumor pharmacodynamics
Preclinical efficacy exposure
DF83 regimen: DT2216 15 mg/kg IP every four days with paclitaxel. Current manuscript: Flank treatment stopped at day 138, followed by 100 days without regrowth. Limitation: The mouse dose and route cannot be converted directly into the human RP2D.
Human Phase 1 pharmacodynamics
Human regimen: DT2216 0.40 mg/kg IV twice weekly. Measured effect: Rapid and persistent BCL-XL reduction in peripheral leukocytes in four patients at the RP2D. Remaining question: Tumor exposure and BCL-XL degradation have not been demonstrated in patients.
The ovarian study measures BCL-XL in PBMCs and evaluates tumor genetics and pro- and anti-apoptotic proteins.
Sources: Qin et al., submitted manuscript, July 2026; Mahadevan et al., J Hematol Oncol 2025; company Phase 1b/2 protocol.

## Slide 31
Key questions for the ovarian program
1
Does the human regimen reproduce tumor BCL-XL degradation?
Phase 1 showed peripheral-leukocyte pharmacodynamics. The Phase 1b tumor assays are intended to test the remaining translation question.
2
Does the combination improve meaningfully on weekly paclitaxel?
The Phase 2 study is single arm. Response rate, durability and PFS will be interpreted against historical experience rather than a randomized control.
3
Can dose intensity be maintained through repeated cycles?
DT2216 caused transient thrombocytopenia in Phase 1. Combination tolerability and recovery kinetics remain central clinical endpoints.
4
Is the preclinical effect reproducible in patients?
The submitted manuscript includes multiple ovarian models, but the durable regressions remain preclinical and the manuscript is not yet peer reviewed.
Sources: Mahadevan et al., J Hematol Oncol 2025; Qin et al., submitted manuscript, July 2026; company Phase 1b/2 protocol.
